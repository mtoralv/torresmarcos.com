Blackjack Monte Carlo engine

A Blackjack simulator in Java that plays millions of hands to measure strategies, card counting and betting systems, with a Jupyter notebook for the analysis.

Why

I wanted to check empirically how the different strategies compare, and whether a player can get an edge over the house or Blackjack is simply a losing game. So I built the game first and then replaced the player with code that plays millions of hands.

  • Engine: 6 decks by default, with splits, doubles, surrender and the usual table rules, all configurable from the command line.
  • Strategies: how to play (basic strategy and a few naive ones) and how much to bet (flat, Hi-Lo counting, Martingale, Oscar’s Grind, 1-3-2-6) are separate, so any pair can be simulated. The terminal game is just one more playing strategy that reads from the keyboard.
  • Results: basic strategy gives a house edge of 0.37 %, which matches the published value for these rules. Hi-Lo counting turns it slightly positive. Betting systems don’t change the return, only the risk: with a 100-unit bankroll, 97 % of Martingale players go broke within 5,000 hands.

What I learned

  • A Monte Carlo result is only useful with its margin of error. A house edge of 0.37 % is smaller than the noise of a few thousand hands, so I needed millions of hands and 95 % confidence intervals to tell strategies apart.
  • House edge and player return are different numbers. Changing the bet size can’t change what you lose per hand played; it only changes how fast you win or lose.
  • The rules matter as much as the strategy: paying 6:5 instead of 3:2 for a blackjack multiplies the house edge by almost five, and card counting gains less when the shoe is reshuffled earlier.
  • Comparing my results with published values is a good way to test a simulator: when the house edge matched, I knew the rules were right.
  • Fixing the random seed makes every simulation reproducible, which made bugs much easier to track down.
  • Exporting the results to CSV and analysing them with pandas and matplotlib in a Jupyter notebook.
